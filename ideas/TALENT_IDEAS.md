# Court Wizard - Talent Ideas

Every spell has a **talent tree** with **3 tiers** of **3 choices each** (9 talents per spell, 279 total). Players pick ONE talent per tier as they gain mastery with each spell. Each tier should be progressively more transformative, turning a familiar spell into something that plays differently.

**Design philosophy**: Talents are **sidegrades**, not pure upgrades. They change *how* a spell works, not just make it better. A talent should make you say "oh, I could build around that" rather than "obviously I pick the biggest number."

## Design Notes

### Talent Philosophy
- **Tier 1**: Small numerical tweaks or quality-of-life changes. Safe picks that refine the spell's existing identity.
- **Tier 2**: Meaningful additions that add new capabilities or change when/how you want to use the spell. Sidegrades with trade-offs.
- **Tier 3**: Transformative options that fundamentally change the spell's role or behavior. Some are powerful but narrow, others are flashy but situational.

### Balancing Guidelines
- Every tier should have at least one option that appeals to each playstyle: aggressive, defensive, and utility-focused.
- Talents that increase power should come with trade-offs (higher cost, shorter duration, smaller radius, etc.).
- The "best" talent in each tier should change depending on what other spells are in the player's loadout.
- Synergies between spell talents (e.g., Fireball's "Chain Ignition" + Mark of Death's "Spreading Blight") should emerge naturally rather than being forced.

### Progress Scaling
- Thresholds are designed so that:
  - **Tier 1** is reachable after 2-4 games of moderate spell usage.
  - **Tier 2** requires sustained commitment to the spell across many games.
  - **Tier 3** represents mastery -- the player has used this spell extensively and earned the right to warp it.
- Some spells have lower thresholds if their metric increments slowly (e.g., Banishment at 10/35/100) and higher if the metric accumulates fast (e.g., Squall at 80/300/900).
